For the last few years, the assumption has been that better models solve the adoption problem. McKinsey's data says otherwise. According to the research, what's scarce isn't AI talent in the narrow sense of people who can train models. It's engineers who understand algorithms, data structures, and system design fundamentals well enough to build something that survives contact with production traffic and legacy systems. The implication, per the source, is that incremental gains in model quality are worth less right now than expertise in scalability and infrastructure. That's a notable inversion of the last few years' hiring priorities, and it echoes the IEEE's separate finding on document processing failures, suggesting the industry's next hard problem isn't smarter models, it's sturdier systems.
